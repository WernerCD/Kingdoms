## Overview

Expanded Kingdoms is a custom Magic: The Gathering variant based on the popular Commander/EDH variant "Kingdoms." It is a **5+ player format** (playable with 4) where each player receives a specialized role card with unique win/loss conditions and special abilities usable once per game.

Similar to normal Kingdoms: there is a King, a Knight, and two Bandit roles. The King wants to survive as the last player standing; the Knight is his ally. The Bandits win when the King dies. All roles are **secret**, so the King can't trust anyone.

---

## Role Types

**King** — Wants to survive as last player standing. Can tap and exile a card from hand (or pay 4 colorless) to choose effects like giving another player mana, drawing cards, or gaining life.

**Knight** — Allied with the King; wins with the King. Has unique randomized abilities usable once per game.

**Bandits (×2)** — Win when the King dies. Each has distinct special abilities varying by role card.

**Renegade** (5th player) — Wild card with unique win conditions that disrupt the standard power balance.

**Nobles** (6+ players) — A second opposing team. Their goal is to be the last players standing, but they **cannot let the King die until the Bandits are eliminated first**. This creates a tentative alliance with the King that must eventually be broken.

## Deck Building Rule

> **The only rule that affects deck-building:** You can only win the game using the win conditions on your role card.

- Cards that say "You win the game" (Laboratory Maniac, Test of Endurance) have **no effect**.
- Cards that say "You can't win the game" (Platinum Angel, Abyssal Persecutor) have **no effect**.

---

## Start of Game (Updated July 2018)

- Players **choose decks AFTER** receiving their role cards (so they aren't stuck with a deck that doesn't suit their role).
- **Bandit Reveal:** All players close their eyes. Bandits briefly open their eyes to identify each other, then close them. All players open their eyes. (Bandits know who each other are from the start.)
- Noble cards are typically revealed before shuffling so players know which Nobles are in the game. Optional rule: play without the reveal (required if The Spymaster is excluded, as he is too powerful if players are unaware of him).

---

## Key Design Notes

- Format is stable up to **at least 9 players**.
- Play time is considerably shorter than free-for-all Commander at the same player count.
- The format resolves through hidden information, political lying, and role-based win conditions.
- The "epic betrayal" — Nobles eventually turning on the King — is a core feature of 6+ player games.

## Role Mapping to Advanced Kingdoms v1.56

| Expanded Kingdoms Role | Border Color | Advanced Kingdoms v1.56 Equivalent |
|------------------------|-------------|-------------------------------------|
| King                   | —           | King (unchanged)                    |
| Knight                 | Green       | Knight (unchanged)                  |
| Bandits                | Red         | **Assassins**                       |
| Nobles                 | Blue        | **Bandits**                         |
| Renegades              | Black       | Renegades (unchanged)               |

> Nobles had to kill Bandits (Assassins) first, THEN the King — same order requirement as current Bandits, but without knowing teammates beforehand.
